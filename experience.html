Case Studies

Each study is written as a specification: the problem in one line, the method used, and what changed when it shipped. Numbers are given where they were actually measured, and omitted where they were not.

Study.002 Airbnb — the anxious gap

ProblemAirbnb's after-you-book, before-you-check-in window made anxious users work too hard to find the one thing they needed next.

MethodRebuilt the reservation screen around a single time-aware next step; sequenced everything else beneath it.

ResultSupport contacts in the pre-stay window fell in testing; the screen now answers "what now?" without a tap.

Study.001 Namma Transit — offline ticketing

ProblemRiders on patchy networks could not buy a ticket the moment the bus arrived; the queue punished every dropped connection.

MethodMoved fare logic to the device and let tickets issue offline, reconciling when signal returned.

ResultShipped March 2026; boarding no longer waits on a live connection. [ placeholder ]

Study.003 Shopkeeper books — list density

ProblemA bookkeeping app for small shops showed too few rows per screen, so owners scrolled instead of scanning during the evening rush.

MethodAdded a user-set compact density and tuned the type for tabular scanning.

ResultShopkeepers chose compact roughly four to one. [ placeholder ]

Study.004 Fieldnotes — capture on the walk

ProblemFilm photographers wanted to log exposure and location without breaking the walk; every extra tap lost the frame.

MethodOne-thumb capture with sensible defaults, editable later at rest.

ResultReleased 2024 as a side project; 3,000 users in the first year. [ placeholder ]

Sec.04 · The operator

About Me

I am a product designer and photographer working out of Bengaluru. I spent my first years after engineering college building interfaces for logistics software, which taught me that the most important screen is usually the one a tired person sees at the worst possible moment. I have designed for that person ever since.

The photography is not a hobby bolted onto the portfolio; it is where the design habits come from. Walking with a camera trains you to notice how people actually move through a market, a platform, a queue — and product work is mostly queues. When a project involves field research, I bring the camera and the discount it buys you on people's patience.

I work best on problems with physical consequences: transit, small commerce, tools for other makers. I am slow to add features and quick to remove steps.

Service history

  • 2026 Independent practice. Namma Transit offline ticketing shipped in March.
  • 2024 Fieldnotes, a capture tool for film photographers, released as a side project; 3,000 users in the first year.
  • 2022 Senior product designer, logistics SaaS. Led the driver-app redesign across 11 languages.
  • 2019 First design role, after two years as a front-end developer. Kept the habit of shipping my own CSS.
