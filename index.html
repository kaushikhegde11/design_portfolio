Case Studies

Each study is written as a specification: the problem in one line, the method used, and what changed when it shipped. Numbers are given where they were actually measured, and omitted where they were not.

Study.002 Airbnb — the anxious gap →

ProblemAirbnb's after-you-book, before-you-check-in window made anxious users work too hard to find the one thing they needed next.

MethodRebuilt the reservation screen around a single time-aware next step; sequenced everything else beneath it.

ResultSupport contacts in the pre-stay window fell in testing; the screen now answers "what now?" without a tap.

Read the full case study →

Study.001 Namma Transit — offline ticketing →

ProblemRiders on patchy networks could not buy a ticket the moment the bus arrived; the queue punished every dropped connection.

MethodMoved fare logic to the device and let tickets issue offline, reconciling when signal returned.

ResultShipped March 2026; boarding no longer waits on a live connection.

Read the full case study →

Study.003 Shopkeeper books — list density

ProblemA bookkeeping app for small shops showed too few rows per screen, so owners scrolled instead of scanning during the evening rush.

MethodAdded a user-set compact density and tuned the type for tabular scanning.

ResultShopkeepers chose compact roughly four to one. [ placeholder ]

Study.004 Fieldnotes — capture on the walk

ProblemFilm photographers wanted to log exposure and location without breaking the walk; every extra tap lost the frame.

MethodOne-thumb capture with sensible defaults, editable later at rest.

Sec.04 · The operator

About Me

I am a UI designer and front-end developer in Bengaluru. I trained as a mechanical engineer. I spent three and a half years in supply chain and material planning at Volvo Auto India Pvt Ltd. That work ran on spreadsheets and dashboards, and on tools built with little thought for the person who used them. That frustration moved me into design.

Manufacturing taught me to respect constraints and to trace a problem to its cause, not decorate the symptom. I bring that habit to interfaces. I understand the flow first, then I draw the screen. I like the full span of the work: I talk to users, structure the information, lay out the UI, and build it in the browser so the design holds up in real code.

Right now I look for a full-time role as a UI designer and front-end developer. I also take freelance work. I am slow to add features and quick to remove steps.
